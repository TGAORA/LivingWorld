# What Should We Do With The Money?

> *Visibility over perfection. Trust over optimization.*

This charter defines how money, labor, and charity interact within Living World MMO.

---

## 1. Core Purpose

A for-profit, charity-oriented project where revenue sustains the world, compensates builders, and directs surplus to charity.

---

## 2. Revenue Sources

Allowed:

* Base game sales
* Expansions / DLC (regions, narratives, eras, systems)
* Optional cosmetic content

Disallowed:

* Pay-to-win
* Power, safety, or progression advantages

---

## 3. Charitable Flow (Canonical)

### Published Revenue Breakdown

Regularly published, high-level allocation across:

* Infrastructure
* In-house compensation
* Open-source compensation
* Charitable allocation

Percentages are not fixed. **Visibility is required.**

---

### Charity Selection

* Public
* Rotatable
* **St. Jude Children’s Research Hospital** as anchor

---

### Project-Affiliated Nonprofit (Subsidy)

A legally separate nonprofit aligned with the project, focused on:

* Long-term coma and reduced-consciousness support
* Extended life-support care
* Family assistance and dignity advocacy

Funding flows are public. The nonprofit has no influence on game design.

---

## 4. Contributor Model

Hybrid in-house + compensated open source. This is not unpaid volunteer labor.

---

## 5. Identity & Anonymity

Public, pseudonymous, or anonymous (via charity assignment).

---

## 6. Contribution Records

Knowledge logs via personal channels. Ownership remains with contributors.

---

## 7. Time × Value Model

Compensation reflects both documented effort and delivered impact.

---

## 8. Quality Validation

Community signal, studio oversight, and future AI assistance.

---

## 9. DLC & Ongoing Contribution

Paid expansions emphasize expression, not power. All players can see content; purchasers can use specific aesthetics.

---

## Closing

Labor, money, and charity are treated with the same transparency and memory as the world itself.
